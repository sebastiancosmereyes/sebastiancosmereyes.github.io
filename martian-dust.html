Martian Dust Mitigation Research

Early-stage research into whether rotating-cylinder flow could help keep dust off solar panels on Mars. This page reflects work in progress — not a proven result.

Role
Research lead
Status
Ongoing
Method
Rotating cylinder–flat plate PIV
Program
Cal Poly SLO

Objective

Dust accumulation is one of the practical limits on solar-panel performance for Mars missions. This project is investigating whether rotating-cylinder flow — the same flow physics studied in the water-tunnel project on this site — could offer a mechanical, low-maintenance way to reduce dust buildup on a panel surface.

Context & scope

This is an early-stage, exploratory research project at Cal Poly SLO. The current scope is establishing the experimental requirements: understanding Martian wind conditions, dust-lifting thresholds, and deposition patterns well enough to design a meaningful rotating-cylinder–flat-plate PIV experiment. No mitigation results exist yet — this page will be updated as the experiment moves forward.

My role & contributions

I'm leading this research. My work so far has focused on characterizing the Martian environmental conditions relevant to the experiment and reviewing the literature on rotating cylinder–flat plate flow configurations to inform the experimental design.

Engineering approach & tools

  • Characterizing Martian wind conditions and seasonal variation
  • Establishing dust-lifting thresholds and particle-force estimates
  • Studying annual dust-deposition patterns relevant to panel performance
  • Literature review of rotating cylinder–flat plate configurations across Reynolds numbers and reduced frequencies
  • Developing a rotating-cylinder–flat-plate PIV experiment based on the above
