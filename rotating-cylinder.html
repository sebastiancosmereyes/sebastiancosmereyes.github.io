Water Tunnel Rotating Cylinder Research

A purpose-built rotating-cylinder apparatus and PIV study connecting classical Magnus-effect theory to real, measured wake behavior in a water tunnel.

Role
Research lead
Max speed
100 RPM
Tools
Arduino, PIV, CAD, fabrication
Program
Cal Poly SLO

Objective

The goal was to design and build a variable-speed rotating-cylinder apparatus for a low-speed water tunnel, then use it to characterize the circulation, lift, drag, and wake behavior associated with the Magnus effect — and check those measurements against classical inviscid-flow and Kutta-Joukowski theory.

Context & scope

This was a Cal Poly SLO research project spanning apparatus design, fabrication, controls, and experimental testing. The rotating-cylinder configuration is also the same underlying flow physics behind the later Martian dust-mitigation research on this site — this project built the apparatus and measurement approach that made that follow-on work possible.

My role & contributions

I led this research. I designed and fabricated the rotating-cylinder apparatus, developed the Arduino-controlled stepper-motor and belt-drive system for precise speed control up to 100 RPM, and ran the PIV analysis used to characterize the resulting flow field.

Engineering approach & tools

  • CAD design of the cylinder, mounting, and drive system
  • Arduino-controlled stepper motor with belt drive, precise to 100 RPM
  • Fabrication of the apparatus for mounting in the water tunnel
  • Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV) to capture the surrounding flow field
  • Post-processing to extract circulation, vorticity, lift, and drag
  • Comparison against inviscid-flow and Kutta-Joukowski predictions
