Bell X-1 Subsonic & Supersonic Aerodynamic Analysis

A reconstruction of the Bell X-1's aerodynamics from Mach 0 through 1.45, built to connect classical low-order methods with real historical flight-test conditions.

Role
Individual project
Speed range
Mach 0–1.45
Tools
MATLAB
Program
Cal Poly SLO, independent study

Objective

The goal was to reverse-engineer the Bell X-1's external geometry and build a MATLAB-based aerodynamic model capable of predicting its lift, drag, and moment behavior across the full speed range it actually flew — from low-subsonic takeoff conditions through its record-setting supersonic dash to Mach 1.45.

Context & scope

The Bell X-1 is a useful validation case because detailed historical wind-tunnel data exists to check predictions against, and because it crosses the transonic regime — one of the harder regions to model well with classical methods. The scope was analytical and computational: geometry reconstruction and aerodynamic modeling, not physical testing.

My role & contributions

I worked on this independently. I reverse-engineered the aircraft's geometry from available reference drawings, wrote the MATLAB analysis pipeline from the panel method through the supersonic wave-drag calculations, and ran the Mach sweep and validation comparisons myself.

Engineering approach & tools

The analysis combined several classical aerodynamic methods, chosen to match the flow regime at each Mach number:

  • Panel methods for subsonic lift and pressure distribution
  • Compressibility corrections through the transonic range
  • Linearized supersonic theory above Mach 1
  • Pressure integration to recover lift, drag, and moment
  • Area-distribution (Sears-Haack-style) methods for wave drag
  • Conical-flow methods for supersonic nose and fuselage effects
